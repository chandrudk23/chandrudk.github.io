Yumzy

Food Delivery Mobile Application

A food delivery app designed to make ordering fast, simple, and enjoyable. The focus was on a clear user flow — from restaurant discovery through to order confirmation — with an interface that stays easy to navigate at every step.

01

Overview

Yumzy is a food delivery mobile app project. The goal was to design a complete end-to-end ordering experience — from discovering restaurants to tracking a completed order — with a clean, accessible interface that doesn't overload the user at any stage.

This was a self-initiated project focused on practising user research, user flow definition, wireframing, and high-fidelity UI production in Figma.

02

Context & Problem

Food delivery apps often fail users in predictable ways: too many steps to reach a food item, cluttered home screens with excessive promotions, unclear restaurant filtering, and checkout flows that feel slow or uncertain.

The challenge for Yumzy was to create an ordering experience that felt genuinely quick — not just visually fast, but structurally simple — so users could find what they want, confirm an order, and feel confident about it with minimal effort.

03

Understanding the Experience

Before wireframing, I reviewed existing food delivery apps to understand common friction points. The key patterns I identified:

  • Overloaded home screens — too many promotional banners competing for attention, pushing actual restaurant listings below the fold.
  • Unclear filtering — category and cuisine filters are often buried or require multiple taps to apply.
  • Checkout drop-off — multi-step checkouts with unclear progress, particularly around delivery address confirmation and payment.
  • Order status ambiguity — post-order screens frequently lack clear, reassuring feedback about order acceptance.

These observations shaped the structural priorities for Yumzy: a focused home screen, prominent filtering, and a checkout flow with clear progress.

04

Information Architecture

The app is structured around four primary destinations accessible from a bottom navigation bar: Home, Search, Orders, and Profile. This keeps the navigation predictable and avoids deep nesting.

07

Final UI

The final high-fidelity screens were designed in Figma with a focus on clear typographic hierarchy, accessible touch targets, and a consistent component system.

The colour system uses a warm white base (#FAFAFA) with a bold primary accent for interactive elements (call-to-action buttons, active states) and a neutral grey palette for supporting text and card surfaces. The intent was a clean, food-positive visual tone without the clutter of excessive promotions or decorative elements.

09

Reflection & Takeaways

Yumzy was a useful project for practising the discipline of keeping an interface genuinely simple rather than just visually minimal. The biggest design challenge was the home screen — balancing discoverability (showing enough to help users find something) with focus (not overwhelming them with options immediately).

The checkout flow taught me to think more carefully about micro-copy: small label decisions (like “Confirm & Pay” vs “Place Order”) meaningfully affect how confident a user feels at the critical moment of committing to a purchase.

If I were to continue developing this project, I would prototype the checkout flow for usability testing to identify where users hesitate, and refine the order tracking screen to provide better real-time feedback.
